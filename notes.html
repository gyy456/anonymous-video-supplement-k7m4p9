01 · RECONSTRUCTION

Direct Gaussian Predictors in Multi-Panorama Scenes

Direct Gaussian predictors are a useful initialization option, but their native input contract matters. PanoSplatt3R predicts from two complete panoramas; our target scenes can contain around 20 panoramas distributed across multiple rooms.

Why this matters for scene-scale use. Applying a two-panorama predictor repeatedly would produce local Gaussian sets in different coordinate frames. Combining them requires pairwise pose and scale recovery, point-cloud or Gaussian alignment, and a globally consistent fusion step. With many panoramas, small relative-pose errors can accumulate across pairs, particularly when overlap is weak or the scene crosses room boundaries. Our framework instead maintains a scene-level coordinate frame during multi-panorama reconstruction and optimization.

The comparison below uses two carefully selected overlapping panoramas and matched target cameras. Even in this favorable two-view setting, PanoSplatt3R retains the coarse room layout while showing visible geometric stretching, bending, and locally wavy boundaries around the doorway, bed, and window. These artifacts motivate subsequent scene-level optimization rather than treating a direct two-view prediction as the finished multi-room representation.

PanoSplatt3R uses two overlapping original panoramas and the released checkpoint. Known input-camera poses align its native first-camera coordinate frame and recover metric scale; target images are not used to fit the Gaussians or camera alignment. The full input pair, camera path, and alignment protocol are documented on the comparison analysis page.

These observations identify a challenge for pairwise prediction in sparse multi-room scenes. The methods use different input coverage and training conditions, so this is a qualitative comparison, not an equal-input benchmark or a general ranking of LRM architectures.

NoPoSplat uses its released two-view checkpoint and the input-pose coordinate frame with unit-baseline scale recovery. Its two perspective inputs have narrower coverage than the complete panoramas; the later bedroom views extend beyond their shared input coverage.

How it could fit into the framework

  1. Predict: obtain Gaussian representations from panorama pairs.
  2. Align and fuse: resolve pairwise coordinate frames and scale, then combine overlapping predictions.
  3. Optimize: refine the scene jointly using multi-view observations and geometric correspondence constraints.
  4. Complete and refine: apply the image–video restoration and distillation stages.

Our DA3-based initialization also supplies dense correspondences used during geometric optimization. Replacing it therefore requires considering both the initial Gaussians and the constraints used to refine them. A direct-predictor initialization followed by our optimization is a promising extension; this hybrid has not yet been evaluated.

Annotation density proxy and category distribution

Using the horizontal convex hull of camera positions as a coverage-area proxy, the median retained-instance density is 2.11 instances/m² (IQR 1.58–2.71). This is not physical object density per unit floor area: camera coverage does not measure complete room area, and annotations are pipeline-fused predictions rather than a manual object census.

The most frequent categories are window (16,101), cabinet (13,246), pillow (10,989), picture (7,945), and curtain (7,621). Less frequent categories include washing machine (154), clock (124), trash can (80), radio (60), and microwave (34). The categories therefore have a long-tailed frequency distribution.

03 · ANNOTATION QUALITY

Verification outcomes and failure sources

Geometric filtering checks multi-view support, box dimensions, and projection consistency. Semantic verification uses multi-view object crops to detect incorrect categories and duplicate instances. Among 161,586 fused candidates entering VLM verification, 127,980 were retained, 33,586 rejected, and 20 flagged for review.

With cross-view merging enabled, adding verification in Table 6 raises F1@0.25 from 18.14 to 19.42 and F1@0.50 from 9.05 to 9.71. These ablations measure its effect on grounding performance; filtering counts alone are not annotation-accuracy estimates.

Unreliable predictions can arise from reconstruction artifacts, occlusion or poorly supported viewpoints, category errors, and inaccurate 3D boxes. The current audit records verification decisions rather than causal labels, so it does not quantify the share attributable to 3DGS, Qwen, or Boxer. Figure 8 provides qualitative evidence of structural artifacts and their reduction through combined image and video restoration.
